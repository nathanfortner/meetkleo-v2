<h2>5. Step-by-Step Exercises to Roll Your R</h2>
<p>Can't roll your R yet? Try these progressive exercises:</p>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Find the spot</b>: Say "la la la" and notice where your tongue touches the roof of your mouth. That's where your R will be made.</li>
		<li><b>Practice the tap</b>: Say "butter" and "ladder" quickly in American English. That middle sound is your tap R. Now try saying "pero" and "caro."</li>
		<li><b>Build to the trill</b>: Say "tee-dee-va" repeatedly, getting faster. The "d" position is very close to the rolled R position.</li>
		<li><b>Add air flow</b>: While your tongue is in the "d" position, try to push air through to make your tongue vibrate.</li>
		<li><b>Practice with "pot of tea"</b>: Say "pot of tea" quickly and repeatedly. Some people find this naturally produces a trill-like sound.</li>
	</ol>
<p><span class="highlighter">Remember: the rolled R is a relaxed vibration, not a forced sound</span>. Your tongue should flutter easily, not be tense.</p>
		<br />

<h2>6. Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Using the English R</b>: The English R is made in the back of the throat. Spanish R's are made with the tip of the tongue at the front of the mouth.</li>
		<li><b>Tensing your tongue</b>: A tense tongue can't vibrate. Stay relaxed!</li>
		<li><b>Giving up too soon</b>: The rolled R often takes weeks or months of practice. Be patient with yourself.</li>
	</ol>
