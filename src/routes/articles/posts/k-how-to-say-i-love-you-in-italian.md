	<p><span class="highlighter">"Ti amo" is reserved exclusively for romantic, passionate love</span>. Using it with family members would sound very strange (and uncomfortable) to Italian ears!</p>
	<p><span class="highlighter">"Ti voglio bene" (literally: "I want good for you") is used for deep affection</span> toward family, close friends, and even pets. It's warm and loving, but not romantic.</p>

	<h2>3. When to Use Each Expression</h2>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">Use "Ti amo" for:</span>

		<li>Pets (Italians love their animals!)</li>
		<li>Anyone you have deep affection for (non-romantically)</li>
	</ul>

	<h2>4. Common Usage Examples</h2>
	<p>Here's how Italians typically express love:</p>
	<ul>
		<li><b>To your partner</b>: "Ti amo, amore mio" (I love you, my love)</li>
		<li><b>To your mother</b>: "Mamma, ti voglio bene" (Mom, I love you)</li>
		<li><b>To your best friend</b>: "Ti voglio tanto bene!" (I love you so much!)</li>
		<li><b>To your child</b>: "Ti voglio bene, tesoro" (I love you, treasure)</li>
	</ul>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
	<h2>5. Pronunciation Tips</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>"Ti" = "tee"</b>: Short and crisp.</li>
		<li><b>"Amo" = "AH-moh"</b>: Stress on the first syllable.</li>
		<li><b>"Voglio" = "VOH-lyoh"</b>: The GLI makes the "LY" sound (like in "million").</li>
		<li><b>"Bene" = "BEH-neh"</b>: Both E's are pronounced.</li>
	</ol>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
<h2>6. Cultural Note</h2>
<p>Italians are generally more expressive with affection than many other cultures. <span class="highlighter">Saying "ti voglio bene" to close friends and family is common and expected</span> - it's not considered overly emotional or unusual.</p>
<p>However, "ti amo" carries significant weight. In Italian romantic culture, saying "ti amo" for the first time is a meaningful milestone in a relationship.</p>
