	<p><span class="highlighter">"Te quiero" literally means "I want you" but is used as "I love you" in a warm, affectionate way</span>. It's appropriate for family, close friends, and the early stages of a romantic relationship.</p>
	<p><span class="highlighter">"Te amo" carries more weight and is reserved for deep, passionate romantic love</span>. Using it too early in a relationship might seem overly intense!</p>

	<h2>4. Regional Differences</h2>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">The te quiero / te amo distinction varies by region</span>
	</div>
	<ul>
		<li><b>Spain</b>: "Te quiero" is the standard even in serious relationships. "Te amo" can sound theatrical.</li>
		<li><b>Mexico</b>: Both are used, with "te amo" reserved for deep romantic love.</li>
		<li><b>Argentina</b>: "Te quiero" is more common; "te amo" is very serious.</li>
		<li><b>Caribbean</b>: Both are used freely, with regional variations.</li>
	</ul>
	<p><span class="highlighter">When in doubt, "te quiero" is always a safe choice</span>. It expresses genuine love without the intensity that might feel premature.</p>
		<br />

	<h2>5. Saying "I Love You" to Family</h2>
	<p>In Spanish-speaking families, expressions of love are common and expected:</p>
	<ul>
		<li><b>To parents</b>: "Te quiero, mamá/papá"</li>
		<li><b>To children</b>: "Te quiero, mi hijo/hija" (my son/daughter)</li>
		<li><b>To siblings</b>: "Te quiero, hermano/hermana"</li>
		<li><b>To grandparents</b>: "Te quiero, abuela/abuelo"</li>
	</ul>
	<p>Using "te amo" with family is less common but not wrong - it simply carries more emotional weight.</p>

<h2>6. Pronunciation Tips</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>"Te" rhymes with "day"</b>: It's a short, crisp sound.</li>
		<li><b>"Quiero" = "KYEH-roh"</b>: The "qu" makes a "k" sound, and the "ie" blends together.</li>
		<li><b>"Amo" = "AH-moh"</b>: Simple and clean - don't add extra sounds.</li>
	</ol>
