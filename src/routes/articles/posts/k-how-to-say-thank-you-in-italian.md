	<h2>4. Pronunciation Tips</h2>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">The "ZI" in "grazie" sounds like "TSEE"</span>
	</div>
	<p>The Italian Z often makes a "TS" sound. So "grazie" is pronounced "GRAHT-see-eh" (not "grah-zee").</p>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">Don't forget the final "E"</span>
	</div>
	<p>Unlike English, every letter in Italian is pronounced. The final "e" in "grazie" should be heard clearly: "GRAHT-see-eh."</p>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
	<h2>5. The Many Uses of "Prego"</h2>
	<p>"Prego" is one of the most versatile words in Italian. It can mean:</p>
	<ul>
		<li><b>You're welcome</b>: As a response to "grazie"</li>
		<li><b>Please, go ahead</b>: When inviting someone to enter or proceed</li>
		<li><b>Here you go</b>: When handing something to someone</li>
		<li><b>May I help you?</b>: In shops and restaurants</li>
		<li><b>Pardon?</b>: When you didn't hear something</li>
	</ul>
	<p><span class="highlighter">Context will always make clear which meaning is intended</span>.</p>
		<br />

<h2>6. Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Don't say "grazzi"</b>: It's "grazie" with an E at the end.</li>
		<li><b>Don't pronounce it like "grat-zee"</b>: The Z is a "ts" sound.</li>
		<li><b>Don't skip "prego" when someone thanks you</b>: Italians expect a response!</li>
	</ol>
