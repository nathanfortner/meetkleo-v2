<h2>5. Exercises to Learn the Italian R</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Find the position</b>: Say "la la la" and notice where your tongue touches. That's where your R will be made.</li>
		<li><b>The "butter" trick</b>: Say "butter" or "ladder" quickly in American English. That middle sound is very close to a single Italian R tap.</li>
		<li><b>The "D" approach</b>: Say "da da da" quickly. Now try to let your tongue bounce or flutter on the "d" position.</li>
		<li><b>Add voice</b>: While in the D position, push air through and try to make your tongue vibrate.</li>
		<li><b>Practice "tre"</b>: The word "tre" (three) is a great practice word because the T sets up your tongue in the right position.</li>
	</ol>
<p><span class="highlighter">The key is relaxation - a tense tongue can't vibrate</span>. Let your tongue flutter naturally.</p>
		<br />

<h2>6. Common Mistakes</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Using the English R</b>: The English R is made in the back of the mouth/throat. Italian R is at the front, with the tongue tip.</li>
		<li><b>Skipping the double RR</b>: In Italian, "caro" and "carro" are different words. Don't rush through double consonants.</li>
		<li><b>Over-trilling</b>: You don't need to roll every R dramatically. Many native speakers use just a tap in casual speech.</li>
	</ol>
