	<h2>4. How to Practice GLI</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Start with English</b>: Say "million" slowly. Focus on the "lli" part.</li>
		<li><b>Isolate the sound</b>: Try to say just the "lli" sound by itself.</li>
		<li><b>Feel your tongue</b>: Your tongue should press flat and wide against the roof of your mouth.</li>
		<li><b>Add Italian words</b>: Now try "figlio" - fee + the "lli" sound + oh.</li>
	</ol>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
	<h2>5. How to Practice GN</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Start with English</b>: Say "canyon" or "onion." The middle sound is your GN.</li>
		<li><b>Isolate the sound</b>: Try to say just the "ny" sound.</li>
		<li><b>Feel your tongue</b>: Your tongue should press against the roof of your mouth, similar to N, but with the middle of your tongue rising up.</li>
		<li><b>Add Italian words</b>: Now try "gnocchi" - start with the "ny" sound, then add "oh-kee."</li>
	</ol>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
<h2>6. Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Don't pronounce G separately in GN</b>: "Gnocchi" is NOT "g-noh-kee." The G is part of the GN sound.</li>
		<li><b>Don't skip the sound</b>: Some learners try to simplify GLI to just "L" or GN to just "N." Italians will notice!</li>
		<li><b>Don't rush</b>: Give these sounds their full value. They're smooth and connected, not hurried.</li>
	</ol>
