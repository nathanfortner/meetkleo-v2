	<h2>3. The Many Uses of "Будь ласка"</h2>
	<p>"Будь ласка" is one of the most useful phrases in Ukrainian. It can mean:</p>
	<ul>
		<li><b>You're welcome</b>: As a response to "дякую"</li>
		<li><b>Please</b>: When making a request</li>
		<li><b>Here you go</b>: When handing something to someone</li>
		<li><b>Go ahead</b>: When inviting someone to proceed</li>
	</ul>
	<p>Context makes the meaning clear in each situation.</p>

	<h2>4. Pronunciation Tips</h2>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">Breaking down "дякую"</span>
	</div>
	<ul>
		<li><b>Д (d)</b> - Like English D</li>
		<li><b>Я (ya)</b> - Like "ya" in "yard"</li>
		<li><b>К (k)</b> - Like English K</li>
		<li><b>У (u)</b> - Like "oo" in "food"</li>
		<li><b>Ю (yu)</b> - Like "you"</li>
	</ul>
	<p>Put it together: <b>D-YA-KOO-YOO</b></p>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
	<h2>5. Cultural Note</h2>
	<p>Ukrainians appreciate politeness and expressing gratitude is important in the culture. <span class="highlighter">Using "дякую" regularly will be noticed and appreciated</span>.</p>
	<p>In more formal situations or when expressing deep gratitude, "щиро дякую" (I sincerely thank you) adds extra warmth and respect.</p>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
<h2>6. Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Don't use Russian "спасибо"</b>: While understood, using the Ukrainian "дякую" shows respect for the language and culture.</li>
		<li><b>Don't skip the "yu" sounds</b>: "Дякую" has two "yu" sounds - don't shorten it to "dyaku."</li>
		<li><b>Don't forget to respond</b>: When someone thanks you, a response like "будь ласка" is expected.</li>
	</ol>
