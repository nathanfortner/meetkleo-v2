	<h2>4. Pronunciation Tips</h2>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">The "GR" in "gracias"</span>
	</div>
	<p>The Spanish "GR" is softer than in English. Don't overemphasize the G - let it flow smoothly into the R.</p>
	<div class="wrapper">
		<span class="vline"></span>
		<span class="callout">The "CI" sounds like "SEE"</span>
	</div>
	<p>In "gracias," the "ci" makes an "s" sound (in Latin America) or "th" sound (in Spain). So it sounds like "GRAH-see-ahs" or "GRAH-thee-ahs."</p>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
<h2>5. Regional Variations</h2>
<p>While "gracias" is universal, you might hear these variations:</p>
	<ul>
		<li><b>Mexico</b>: "Gracias" is standard, but you might hear "mil gracias" more often</li>
		<li><b>Argentina</b>: "Gracias" with distinctive Rioplatense pronunciation</li>
		<li><b>Spain</b>: "Gracias" with the "th" sound for the C</li>
		<li><b>Caribbean</b>: Sometimes shortened casually to just "gracia"</li>
	</ul>
		<br />
	<div class="hline"></div>
<h2>6. Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Don't say "gracías"</b>: The stress is on the first syllable (GRA-cias), not the second.</li>
		<li><b>Don't forget the "s"</b>: "Gracia" (without the s) means "grace" or "charm," not "thanks."</li>
		<li><b>Don't over-pronounce the R</b>: Keep it soft and quick, not a hard rolled R.</li>
	</ol>
