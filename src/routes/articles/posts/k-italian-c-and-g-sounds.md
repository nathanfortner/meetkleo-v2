	<h2>3. The Pattern: A Simple Rule</h2>
	<p>Here's the easy way to remember:</p>
	<table>
		<thead>
			<tr>
				<th>Before...</th>
				<th>C sounds like...</th>
				<th>G sounds like...</th>
			</tr>
		</thead>
		<tbody>
			<tr>
				<td>A, O, U</td>
				<td>K (hard)</td>
				<td>G as in "go" (hard)</td>
			</tr>
			<tr>
				<td>E, I</td>
				<td>CH (soft)</td>
				<td>J (soft)</td>
			</tr>
			<tr>
				<td>H + E/I</td>
				<td>K (hard)</td>
				<td>G as in "go" (hard)</td>
			</tr>
		</tbody>
	</table>
	<p><span class="highlighter">The H acts as a "hardener" - it keeps C and G hard even before E or I</span>.</p>

<h2>5. Tips for Mastery</h2>
	<ol>
		<li><b>Think about the following vowel</b>: Before you pronounce C or G, look at what comes next.</li>
		<li><b>The H is your clue</b>: If you see CH or GH, you know it's a hard sound.</li>
		<li><b>Practice pairs</b>: Compare "amici" and "amiche" to train your ear.</li>
		<li><b>Italian words you know</b>: "Cappuccino" (hard C + soft C), "spaghetti" (hard GH) - you've been practicing Italian without realizing it!</li>
